### The Anatomy of Revenue Leakage in Customer Success

In traditional go-to-market organizations, customer retention fails because of three systemic disconnects:

#### 1. The Post-Sale Context Chasm
During an intensive 90-day sales cycle, the prospect reveals deep operational vulnerabilities, key business metrics, internal stakeholder politics, and implementation timelines. However, once the contract is signed, the deal is handed off to Customer Success via a rushed 30-minute internal meeting or a sparse CRM notes field.

The customer is forced to re-explain their business model and strategic objectives to a new Customer Success Manager (CSM). When onboarding takes 60 to 90 days longer than promised, initial enthusiasm turns into buyer remorse, creating the conditions for first-year churn.

#### 2. Lagging Churn Indicators
Most Customer Success platforms measure customer health using lagging indicators: support ticket escalations, formal NPS survey scores, or overdue invoice notices. 

By the time a customer logs a critical support escalation or complains about pricing 30 days before renewal, the executive decision to replace the vendor has already been finalized. The organization is forced into reactive discounting to preserve the contract.

#### 3. Uncoordinated Expansion Motions
Account expansion (upselling additional licenses, expanding into adjacent business units, cross-selling advanced modules) is frequently conducted through uncoordinated guesswork. Account managers schedule awkward quarterly business reviews (QBRs) where they pitch new features without knowing whether the client has realized value from their initial investment.
